# W07 — Payment, Seat, cancellation and refund contract

This contract freezes the product and integration boundaries for W07. W07.1 and W07.2 server/UI paths are implementing against two additive draft migrations; neither migration has been applied. The return/webhook path is mocked and signed ingress is implemented, but no Stripe call or checkout session has occurred. Live checkout remains disabled unless fulfillment readiness is explicitly configured after the webhook is deployed. This document does not authorize any live Stripe action.

## Product objects and relationships

| Object | Purpose and lifecycle | Relationships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Table Invitation** | A non-expiring offer for one named member to buy one Seat at one Table. It is not a Seat, capacity hold, payment, or app admission. It remains active after a failed/expired checkout hold until separately revoked or the Table closes. The W06 browse/detail contract exposes the server-owned price as 2,500 USD cents. | `app_table_invitations` binds invitation ID, Table head, Guest profile, Auth user, source request, approved proposal, and approving admin. It may lead to at most one Seat. |
| **Checkout Attempt / Hold** | A server-created, temporary capacity allocation for the named invitee while a Stripe card checkout is open. The hold expires after 30 minutes; retrying does not extend an active hold indefinitely. It is not a Seat and does not confirm payment. | New W07 `app_checkout_attempts` references exactly one `app_table_invitations.invitation_id`; Table/Guest/owner derive from that invitation. Its persisted provider session and mode bind to that attempt. |
| **Payment** | A verified Stripe charge fact, including provider account/mode, amount, currency and stable provider identifiers. Processing and a browser return URL are not proof of payment. | New W07 `app_payments` references one `app_checkout_attempts.attempt_id`; each provider payment identifier is unique within its account/mode. A payment may confirm one Seat or require a refund obligation when fulfillment is impossible. |
| **Seat** | The Guest’s durable paid place at a Table. It is created only after server-verified paid state and an atomic allocation succeeds. | New W07 `app_seats` references one invitation and one verified payment, each at most once. Guest/Table derive through the invitation chain. A database constraint and service check enforce at most one active-paid Seat per Table/Guest. |
| **Cancellation Request** | A Guest’s request for staff to remove their active paid Seat. The request is not removal and does not release capacity or paid rights. | Belongs to one Seat. At most one request is pending for a Seat; prior decisions remain history. |
| **Refund Obligation** | The durable amount still owed for a verified payment after an approved cancellation or an unfulfillable late payment. It exists independently of whether Stripe can settle it now. | Belongs to one Payment. The amount owed is bounded by the verified paid balance less amounts already refunded or reserved. |
| **Refund Operation** | An idempotent attempt to settle some or all of an obligation through Stripe. Provider uncertainty is persisted and reconciled; it is never guessed as success. | Reserves a bounded amount against one Payment and uses one stable operation/idempotency key across retry. Multiple operations may settle a partial obligation but their committed allocations cannot exceed the amount owed or paid. |

## W06 schema binding and W07-owned records

W06 has applied migrations `20261113043146_w06_published_tables_and_requests.sql`, `20261113055016_w06_roster_proposals_and_invitations.sql`, and `20261113142334_w06_table_invitation_history_and_delivery.sql`. The real local W06.5 DB harness and intercepted-Mailpit browser journey passed; see [W06.5 evidence](w06.5-browser-slice-evidence.md). W07.1 draft `20261113161438_w07_checkout_holds_and_sessions.sql` defines checkout holds, attempts, payments and Seats. W07.2 draft `20261113181544_w072_payment_fulfillment_and_stripe_inbox.sql` adds a minimal signed-event inbox and late-payment refund obligations. These drafts have not been applied or verified against Supabase.

The authoritative invitation chain is:

```text
app_table_invitations
  .invitation_id       (PK)
  .table_id            -> app_supply_table_heads.table_id
  .guest_id            -> app_member_profiles.guest_id
  .auth_user_id        -> auth.users.id
  .source_request_id   -> app_table_requests.request_id
  .proposal_id         -> app_table_roster_proposals.proposal_id
  .approved_by         -> auth.users.id
```

The associated approved roster member is `app_table_roster_proposal_members(proposal_id, request_id, guest_id)`, whose captured profile and identity versions support stale-version checks. `app_table_roster_proposals` binds its `table_id`, `supply_revision_id`, `state='approved'`, and approval actor/time. The source `app_table_requests` row independently binds `table_id`, `guest_id`, and `auth_user_id` and keeps the request history. These are ordinary individual foreign keys, not a composite constraint proving that all IDs refer to the same person/Table. Every W07 transaction must verify those equalities by joining the invitation to its source request, approved proposal/member, `app_member_profiles` (`auth_user_id`, admission, approval and current identity versions), and `app_supply_table_heads`/published revision. Do not treat possession of an invitation UUID as ownership or rely on the old proposal listing/cursor API to authorize checkout.

W06.5 delivery is a separate concern: `app_table_invitation_outbox` has one row per `(invitation_id,message_kind)` (`invitation` or `reminder_t48`), and `app_table_invitation_attempts` records delivery attempts. Neither relation is a checkout, payment, capacity hold, or Seat. W07 must not place checkout state in that outbox, reuse its operation IDs, or infer payment from its delivery state.

W07 introduces additive, private/service-only relations with this FK direction:

- `app_checkout_attempts.invitation_id -> app_table_invitations.invitation_id`. Each attempt is for exactly one invitation; its Table, Guest and Auth owner are derived from that invitation chain. Repeated begin requests for that invitation reuse its still-live hold; another attempt cannot extend the deadline.
- `app_table_runtime.table_id -> app_supply_table_heads.table_id` is the one-row-per-Table operational state, including cancellation; W08's proceed decision shares this authority. `app_supply_table_heads.lifecycle` and revision pointers remain the supply/publication lifecycle and are not rewritten to mean runtime cancellation/completion. Table cancellation preserves the published supply snapshot for member history.
- `app_payments.attempt_id -> app_checkout_attempts.attempt_id`. A payment is a server-verified successful charge fact, unique to its stored provider payment identifier and mode. Pending/unknown checkout is represented on the attempt/inbox, never as a successful Payment.
- `app_seats.invitation_id -> app_table_invitations.invitation_id` and `app_seats.payment_id -> app_payments.payment_id`, each unique for the Seat relation. Thus one invitation/payment cannot create two Seats; the attempt chain identifies the same invitation and owner. Enforce one active-paid Seat per Table/Guest in the database as well as the Table-locked service path.
- Table cancellation adds a distinct terminal `closed_by_table` state for affected `app_table_requests` (not `declined` or `withdrawn`), closes active `app_table_invitations`, releases live checkout attempts, marks Seats cancelled, and creates the remaining refund obligations. Extend the W06 request-state constraint additively; do not falsify a staff decline or delete application history. Late paid events remain in the ledger and create only an uncovered refund obligation.
- `app_seat_cancellation_requests.seat_id -> app_seats.seat_id`; retain decided requests as history and allow at most one pending request per Seat.
- `app_refund_obligations.payment_id -> app_payments.payment_id`; `app_refund_operations.obligation_id -> app_refund_obligations.obligation_id`. Every allocation is integer USD cents and is reserved against that Payment's remaining verified balance.
- `app_seat_cancellation_requests.seat_id -> app_seats.seat_id`; retain each request with `pending|approved|denied`, request/decision timestamps and a guest-safe denial reason. At most one request may be pending per Seat. W07.3 adds only this private read model; W07.4 owns its mutation RPCs and review flow.
- `app_stripe_event_inbox` uniquely binds each verified provider event by Stripe account/mode and event ID before acknowledgment. It links to an attempt/payment only after trusted stored session/payment identifiers resolve it; unmatched verified paid events stay durably unresolved and visible for reconciliation.
- `app_money_operations` stores actor, operation ID, action, canonical input hash and result for checkout, cancellation, Table cancellation and refund decisions. Replays return the same result; actor/action/body rebinding conflicts.

All new financial/hold/inbox/Seat relations use RLS and no browser writes or direct reads; RPCs/repositories are service-only after the server derives and authorizes the actor. Member/admin APIs return purpose-built safe DTOs. The W07 cancellation/refund service is the only path that removes a paid Seat or creates a refund operation. The provider adapter owns Stripe calls and signature verification; it cannot decide Guest/Table ownership from client parameters or provider metadata alone. The existing Table Invitation email adapter remains separate and must not create an Auth link for Seat checkout.

Lock and protect the existing `app_supply_table_heads` row for every Table-affecting money/Seat mutation. Preserve its `protected_by_activity` latch: once invitation/checkout/paid activity protects a Table, expiry, cancellation, refund settlement or a failed provider call must not clear the latch or allow an old supply revision to be rewritten underneath that activity. The pending W06.3–W06.4 database acceptance also leaves invitation-derived latch verification open; W07 checkout must not rely on an unverified latch as proof of roster validity and must retain its direct revision/identity checks. The runtime row, supply head and invitation state must be changed in one transaction for Table cancellation; do not substitute an Airtable publication field or supply `lifecycle` value.

The older draft [Seat object](../../../knowledge/objects/seat.md) calls hold-vs-confirmed state an implementation detail and leaves refund/cancellation policy open. The current PRD and architecture resolve those points: checkout hold is a distinct allocation object, the Seat is paid, cancellation-requested keeps active-paid rights, and refund decisions are explicit staff actions. Keep the mental-model language “Seat at a Table”; avoid “reservation” and never describe an open offer or hold as a confirmed Seat.

## State boundaries

These are domain transitions; implementation may use separate fields for provider, capacity and business states to avoid conflating them.

### Checkout Attempt / Hold

| Transition | Preconditions and effect | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Begin → held | Authenticated named invitee has an active invitation, approved current identity/profile and roster, an eligible Table, and available capacity. Allocate a 30-minute capacity hold measured by database time. | Replay of the same operation returns the same attempt. A second request cannot extend a still-live hold. |
| Held → consumed | Same payment is verified as paid and the Seat is committed under the Table lock. | Duplicate webhook/return resumes or returns the same fulfillment. |
| Held → released/expired | Checkout definitively fails, is abandoned/expired, or staff closes the Table / revokes the invitation. | Release is idempotent. It cannot downgrade a consumed/paid attempt. Expiry alone never revokes the invitation. |
| Session outcome → unknown | Stripe session creation or expiry outcome is ambiguous. | Keep the capacity state conservative until authoritative session/payment reconciliation. Reuse the original operation key; do not create another session blindly. |

#### Embedded Checkout and bounded provider expiry

The UI uses Stripe Checkout `ui_mode=embedded_page` and initializes it with the owner-only `client_secret` returned by begin. The server creates a card-only USD 25 session and constructs a return URL for the same invitation and attempt, including Stripe’s `{CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}` return placeholder; callers cannot supply a URL. W07.2 return handling must authenticate the member, load the stored attempt, and cross-check that returned Session ID against the bound provider Session before reading status. The query parameter alone is never payment evidence. The secret is transient bearer material: it is never persisted, returned in eligibility/recovery DTOs, logged, audited or included in operation results. A retry first verifies the owner and stored attempt, then retrieves the bound Stripe session for a fresh transient client secret.

The database capacity hold ends exactly 30 minutes after its reservation timestamp. Stripe currently requires `expires_at` to be at least 30 minutes after the session is created, so transport delay makes an exactly aligned deadline unreliable. W07 targets Stripe expiry at the hold deadline plus a **bounded 2-minute grace**. If the provider rejects that target as too soon, the attempt is released with a safe definitive code; no provider call is treated as accepted. Capacity is released at the 30-minute DB deadline. During the 2-minute grace, another guest may take the slot. A late successful payment is durably recorded; fulfillment may consume a slot only if one remains and all current rules pass, otherwise a full remaining-refund obligation is created. Same-invitation retries remain blocked until the old session is authoritatively expired/reconciled. Unknown create/expiry outcomes reuse the same provider idempotency key and canonical body; they never mint another session blindly.

The W07.1 service check is covered at 29:59, exactly 30:00, and 31:59: it may retrieve and return a client secret only before the database hold deadline. At or after the deadline it returns an unknown/recovery state, releases capacity by database time, and does not retrieve or reissue a client secret. The database dispatch claim also refuses to claim a create/replay after `hold_until`.

### W07.2 signed payment fulfillment and recovery

- Subscribe the Stripe webhook endpoint only to `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, and `checkout.session.expired`. Unrelated events are not handled by this slice. Stripe Connect account events are rejected until the provider adapter supports account-scoped retrieval.
- Verify the raw body’s `Stripe-Signature` HMAC before any repository call, with a 300-second timestamp tolerance and constant-time comparison. After signature verification, persist `(provider_mode, provider_account_id, event_id)`, event type, creation time and bound session ID before acknowledging. Never store the raw body, signature or secret. A DB/provider processing failure returns retryable failure while leaving the inbox event replayable. A duplicate terminal event returns its stored outcome; an unfinished event resumes through the same idempotent flow.
- Retrieve the bound Checkout Session through the server Stripe adapter. Verify exact stored session ID, server mode/livemode, `payment` mode, `complete` status, `paid` payment status, 2,500 USD cents, USD currency and a stable PaymentIntent ID. Event metadata, return-query parameters and browser success state are not payment evidence.
- Owner return uses `POST /api/me/table-invitations/{invitationId}/checkout/return` with only `{ attemptId, sessionId }` as untrusted hints. Cookie-authenticated server code verifies the admitted member, then cross-checks invitation owner, attempt owner and the exact stored Session ID before retrieving provider status. Return and webhook call the same transactional fulfillment operation. Return DTOs include only the owner’s attempt/payment/Seat state.
- Both paths lock the Table head first, then profile, invitation and attempt; refresh database time after acquiring locks; and recheck current admission, approved profile/roster/supply versions, active invitation, Table lifecycle/time gate, existing Seat and capacity. A verified Payment creates at most one Seat; Table locking plus unique payment/Seat keys make webhook, return and duplicate replay idempotent.
- A verified late payment after hold expiry/revocation, a failed live eligibility recheck, or full capacity is still durably recorded. If it cannot safely receive a Seat, create a full remaining-refund obligation and return `refund_pending`; never discard the charge or claim payment failed. Unsettled verified payment/refund obligations block another checkout using `payment_unresolved`. Owner recovery remains `refund_pending` until provider-confirmed refund settlement. Only then may the invitation become eligible again.
- An `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` event releases capacity only after a fresh server retrieval confirms the exact bound Session is `complete` and `unpaid` at the expected $25 USD amount. The event row and transition to `released` commit atomically under the Table-first lock order. If current provider truth is paid, use paid fulfillment; if it is inconsistent or unavailable, leave the attempt unresolved and retry/reconcile. A later paid event for a released session is still handled by the same late-payment rule.
- When Stripe confirms an expired/unpaid Session, transition its bound attempt to `expired` under the Table-first lock order. This releases the one-unresolved-attempt latch so the member may begin a new attempt; it does not revoke the Table invitation. A later verified paid event for that old Session is still processed and receives a currently eligible slot or creates the refund obligation.
- `CheckoutEligibilityDTO.provider` exposes only `{ ready, mode, publishableKey }`; the key is public and never a secret. Readiness is false unless configured Stripe mode, matching secret/public key prefixes, webhook signing secret and explicit `CHECKOUT_FULFILLMENT_READY=true` are present. The browser also requires exact equality with its `VITE_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`. Keep the release gate false until webhook deployment and operational readiness are verified.
- Unit/provider/HTTP tests are mocked. The rollback-only DB harness is prepared but intentionally unrun until coordinator review and explicit authorization for the local test stack.

## Payment, Seat and cancellation

- A verified paid Payment plus a valid available slot and current authorization may atomically create one confirmed Seat and consume its attempt.
- A verified charge does not guarantee that fulfillment remains possible. If the hold expired, invitation was revoked, Table was cancelled, current roster/identity rules fail, or no slot remains, persist the Payment and a full remaining-refund obligation. Show **“Payment received; refund pending”**; do not claim failure or discard the charge.
- Seat states exposed to the member/admin can distinguish `confirmed`, `cancellation_requested`, `cancelled` and `completed`. `confirmed`, `cancellation_requested` and `completed` are `active_paid` while not removed/cancelled. A pending cancellation does not remove capacity, check-in, voting, or historical feedback rights. Only confirmed removal/cancellation ends active participation; refund settlement timing does not control that decision.
- A cancellation request resolves to denied or approved. Denial leaves the Seat confirmed and sends a safe explanation. Approval records reviewer, decision, reason and integer refund cents; it releases the Seat and capacity immediately, even when Stripe is unavailable. A refund obligation remains durable if money is owed.
- Table cancellation closes requests/offers and blocks checkout, releases holds and Seats, and creates the full remaining refund obligation for every verified charge. A subsequently observed paid event for that cancelled Table also creates the uncovered full-refund obligation; it never reopens the Table or creates a Seat.

### Refund

An admin decision may owe 0 through the remaining verified paid balance in integer USD cents. “Full” means the remaining balance after prior verified refunds, not an amount above it. `pending`, `submitted`, `unknown` and any failed-but-still-owed allocation remain reserved against available refund balance. The UI may say “Refunded” only after verified provider settlement. A definitive failure leaves the obligation open and retryable; an uncertain response keeps the same operation key and requires provider reconciliation. Repeated cancellation, Table cancellation, webhook delivery and refund-worker retries must not create duplicate or excess refunds.

## Capacity, serialization and idempotency invariants

1. For a Table, `active_paid Seats + live checkout holds <= 5` at every committed state. Active roster membership is also at most five. Invitation or request count alone does not occupy checkout capacity.
2. Serialize all capacity-changing checkout, fulfillment, Seat removal, invitation revocation and Table-cancellation transactions by locking `app_supply_table_heads` for the invitation’s `table_id` first. Then lock affected `app_member_profiles` rows in UUID order, the invitation rows, and then attempt/Seat/payment rows in fixed UUID order. Monetary operations additionally serialize on the Payment row. All competing flows use the same lock order to avoid races/deadlocks.
3. Under those locks, expire/reconcile eligible old holds, revalidate the invitation/source-request/proposal-member identity chain, active invitation state, activated admission, current approved identity version, captured profile version, published supply revision and Table lifecycle/time gate, active Seat, and final slot before creating or consuming capacity. Use database time for deadlines. A stale or mismatched link grants no checkout.
4. Database uniqueness and transaction constraints backstop service checks: unique Stripe event `(account,mode,event_id)`, unique Stripe session/payment/refund identifiers within provider account/mode, unique live attempt per invitation/Guest/Table, one Seat per invitation/payment, one active-paid Seat per Table/Guest, at most one pending cancellation request, and operation-key idempotency. Exact constraint names are chosen in the additive W07 migration while preserving these keys.
5. Authenticated guest actions derive Guest, invitation, amount, currency, environment, return URL and provider session from verified server context. Callers cannot select a Guest, Table, owner, Stripe mode, price, payment state, refund balance or provider ID.
6. Webhooks are signature-verified and durably inserted before acknowledgement. A duplicate event either resumes unfinished handling or returns its prior result. Webhook and verified return use one fulfillment service. A DB failure before durable inbox insert returns retryable failure; an inserted event stays replayable.
7. Every mutation carries an operation UUID where the architecture requires it. Persist `(actor, operationId, action, input_hash, result)`; same key/action/body replays the exact outcome, while a changed binding conflicts. Stripe create/refund calls use durable Stripe idempotency keys derived from the persisted attempt/refund-operation UUID. Never mint a new key for an uncertain prior call.
8. Delivery is independent of payment/Seat truth. A failed confirmation email never unconfirms a Seat or triggers a second charge. Guest UI always reads the durable payment/Seat/refund ledger, never a success query parameter.

### W07.4 cancellation request and decision contract

Member cancellation history uses `MemberCancellationRequestDTO`: `{requestId,seatId,state,version,requestedAt,decidedAt,memberReason,memberSafeReason,refundCents}`. The history response includes server-derived `seatState` (`confirmed|cancellation_requested|cancelled|completed`) and `requestAvailability:{canRequest,reason}`, where reason is `available|request_pending|seat_not_active|payment_unresolved|table_closed`. Runtime completion/cancellation is reflected as terminal `seatState` even while a request remains pending, so the UI does not tell a member that their Seat remains active after the Table closes. Admin detail likewise projects terminal Seat state from current Table runtime if the later lifecycle worker has not yet updated the Seat row. Eligibility is computed from trusted current state; the member UI must not implement its own eligibility rule. A request is allowed for an active confirmed Seat until the Table is completed or cancelled, including while the Table is in progress. Use server Seat/runtime state, not the client clock. The POST repeats all checks transactionally under the Table lock.

The state trace is `confirmed → cancellation_requested` with request `pending/version 1`; both the Seat and its paid capacity/check-in/vote rights remain active. Admin denial moves the request to `denied/version 2`, restores the Seat to the runtime-appropriate state (`confirmed` while the Table remains active, `completed` after completion, or `cancelled` after Table cancellation), stores a safe member-visible rationale, and leaves attendance/vote snapshots unchanged. Admin approval moves request to `approved/version 2`, marks the Seat `cancelled` with a timestamp, revokes the original Table Invitation so it cannot be reused, and stores the selected `refundCents`. A positive refund decision creates one durable `open` obligation in the same transaction; a zero decision creates no obligation. No W07.4 path calls Stripe or claims money settled. A fresh request may be made after denial if the server reports it available; an approved/removed Seat cannot request again.

Request and decision mutations carry `operationId`; a durable operation row binds actor, action, canonical body hash and result. Exact actor/action/body replay returns the recorded result and writes no second request, audit or obligation. A reused ID with a different actor/body conflicts. A new operation against a pending request returns `request_pending`; a terminal decision with another operation returns `decision_finalized`; stale `expectedVersion` conflicts. Cross-member Seat/request IDs return the same `not_found` response as nonexistent IDs.

Request and decision RPCs serialize an operation ID with a transaction-scoped advisory lock, check actor/action/canonical body replay, then acquire business row locks in this order: `app_supply_table_heads FOR UPDATE` → member profile → activated admission (member request only) → invitation → cancellation request → Seat → Payment → refund obligations. The member eligibility helper performs no row locks before the Table lock. Validate active paid payment and request/version state after locks. `refundAvailableCents` is the persisted verified payment amount less all already settled or still owed/reserved refund obligations; failed-but-still-owed amounts remain reserved. All obligation inserts are bounded by that balance. The same transaction writes one redacted audit event for each new request/decision. Approve and deny also insert one idempotent `app_seat_cancellation_notice_outbox` intent (`notice_kind='decision'`, initially queued; unique per request/kind). W07.4 does not send it; W10 owns the email template, attempt history, worker and recovery. No notification intent is created on replay.

W07.4 safe errors are `unauthenticated`, `forbidden`, `not_found`, `invalid_input`, `request_pending`, `seat_not_cancellable`, `stale_version`, `refund_amount_exceeds_balance`, `decision_finalized`, `operation_conflict`, and `temporarily_unavailable`. Domain state/version/balance errors return 409; service/DB outage returns retryable 503. No Table cancellation, replacement, refund worker, provider retry or provider reconciliation is in this slice.

Hosts may read assigned operational Seat/attendance state and flag an issue, but do not read provider/payment details, initiate checkout, approve cancellations or create refunds. Restaurant users receive assigned-venue operations only. Admins may review money state, decide cancellation/refund and resolve provider exceptions. Members see only their own checkout, Seat and refund status.

## Acceptance traceability

| Acceptance | Contract rule to verify |
| --- | --- |
| **A09** | Race two checkout attempts at the final slot; at most one hold wins. Replay request, return and webhook; one Seat and one fulfillment per verified payment. |
| **A10** | Processing, failed and abandoned checkout never displays a confirmed Seat; safe retry/My Seats/support path exists; failed checkout releases hold but leaves invitation active. |
| **A11** | Delayed charge after hold expiry/revocation/Table cancellation either fulfills only after locked current eligibility/capacity checks or records the charge and full remaining-refund obligation. No oversell or lost charge. |
| **A12** | Seat remains confirmed after notification failure; delivery recovery creates no additional charge or Seat. |
| **A13** | One cancellation request; pending request retains capacity and active-paid rights; deny/approve replay is idempotent; removal releases the Seat even if refund settlement is unavailable. |
| **A14** | Concurrent partial/full refund allocations stay within paid balance; retry after timeout uses same operation key; unknown/pending/failed remains visible and never says “Refunded” before settlement. |
| **A15** | Table cancellation atomically closes requests/offers, blocks checkout, releases holds/Seats and records full remaining refund obligations; delayed payment event adds only the uncovered obligation; retries cannot over-refund. |
